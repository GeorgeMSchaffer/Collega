Idea 01 · Continuous Improvement

Standardize the intake checklist

New / Pending since Aug 15 — waiting on a reviewer. Two people have upvoted it; Olivia intends to move it to In Review after standup.
NCWritten by Noah Contributor · Aug 15, 2026 · last edited 3 hours ago

The problem

Three teams each keep their own version of the intake form. Because none of them is authoritative, roughly a third of intake requests come back for missing information — most often the safety sign-off or the affected production line. Each bounce costs about a day and a half of calendar time, and the requester usually has to chase it themselves.

The problem is not that people are careless. It is that the checklist a requester sees depends on which team they happened to ask, and none of those versions has been reconciled since the cell was reconfigured in March.

What we propose

One concise checklist, owned by the operations group, used by all three teams. Concretely:

  • A single form with the fields all three variants share, plus the safety sign-off as a required section.
  • The affected line captured as a selection rather than free text, so it can be reported on later.
  • A short "what happens next" note at the end, so requesters stop asking for status by email.

We would keep it to one page. The failure mode for checklists is length, and the current three exist partly because the original was too long to use.

What it would take

Reconciling the three variants is roughly a half-day workshop with one person from each team. Drafting and circulating the merged version is another day. The real cost is the follow-through — someone has to own it, and the teams have to actually retire their own copies.

Open questions

  • Who owns the checklist once it exists? Operations is the obvious answer but has not agreed.
  • Do we need a variant for external contractors, or does the same form work with one extra field?
  • Does this block the faster-review-path pilot, or can they proceed independently?

Guidance

The structured prompts are the point of this direction. A free-form "Description" box produces one-line ideas that nobody can evaluate; three named questions produce something a reviewer can act on.

Every field is optional except title and type — the structure is a scaffold, not a gate. The published document simply omits the sections you left blank.
