# Writing steps people read

A tour interrupts someone. The copy is what justifies it.

## Two sentences

One naming the thing, one giving the reason to care. Longer and people skim;
shorter and it's a label, not an explanation.

> **The chapter calendar**
> Every meeting, workshop, deadline, and conference date for the season, in one
> place. Officers keep it updated, so what you see here is what's happening.

## Answer "why do I care", not "what is this"

The screen already says what things are. The tour's job is the part that isn't
visible.

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| "This is the search box." | "Looking for one specific thing? Search across every video instead of hunting through the tabs." |
| "Here is your progress." | "Watch a video and it's counted here automatically — on any device you log in from." |
| "This is the resources page." | "Intro decks explaining how each event works, plus templates and study guides." |

## Skip the obvious

A step explaining that a Save button saves teaches people the tour isn't worth
reading — and they'll skim the step that actually mattered. Cut anything a
competent stranger would work out in two seconds.

## Use the app's own words

If the UI says "Business Academy", the tour says "Business Academy". Introducing
a synonym in the tour means the user goes looking for a thing that isn't there.

## Shape the arc

- **Open by orienting.** Where am I, what is this for.
- **Middle: the things they'd otherwise miss** — the feature behind a menu, the
  thing that saves automatically, the filter nobody notices.
- **Close by handing back control.** What to do next, and where the help lives.
  A good last step mentions how to replay the tour.

## Mention what's automatic

People systematically miss things that happen without them: progress saving,
sync across devices, officers keeping something updated. These are the highest-
value steps in most tours because nothing on screen says them.

## Write mobile variants where the UI differs

If the desktop nav bar and the mobile hamburger are the same idea, write both
and tag them by viewport. They are one step told two ways — so show one, and
count one.

## Length

10–20 steps for a whole app; 3–5 for a single feature. Past that people click
Next to make it stop, and a tour that gets skipped teaches nothing.
